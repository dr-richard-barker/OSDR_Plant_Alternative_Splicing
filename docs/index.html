Alternative Splicing in Plant Spaceflight Transcriptomes

A coordinated multi-study alternative splicing (AS) analysis of plant spaceflight transcriptomes. By leveraging 199 samples from 7 Arabidopsis and 1 Brassica studies in the NASA Open Science Data Repository, we reveal post-transcriptional splicing networks responding dynamically to microgravity, cosmic radiation, and extraterrestrial substrates.

Overview

Characterizing post-transcriptional regulation under spaceflight-relevant stresses.

While transcriptional changes in spaceflight have been widely mapped, alternative splicing remains a major frontier. This pipeline leverages rMATS-turbo to quantify alternative splicing events, predicts nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) sensitivity, performs machine learning (UMAP, nested cross-validation, LOSOCV, and SHAP importance), and conducts multi-ontology enrichment analysis (GO, MapMan) to uncover the post-transcriptional footprint of space stress.

01The Spaceflight Splicing Landscape

Retained introns and nonsense-mediated decay coordinate the stress response.

Across spaceflight (ISS), altered gravity, simulated galactic cosmic ray (GCR) radiation, and growth in authentic Apollo lunar regolith, splicing regulation is highly active. Intriguingly, retained introns represent the single largest class of differentially spliced events. NMD predictions indicate that these intron-retention events introduce premature termination codons, acting as an unproductive splicing mechanism to downregulate transcript abundance post-transcriptionally.

02Core Splicing Biomarkers: AT4G17310

Recurrence analysis identifies robust splicing targets across gravity, radiation, and lunar substrates.

Comparing significant genes across all studies identified 93 genes significant in 2 or more studies. Most notably, AT4G17310 is differentially spliced in 4 separate studies spanning fractional gravity, partial gravity, lunar regolith, and galactic cosmic ray radiation—making it the most robust plant spaceflight splicing target known to date. Other key recurrent genes include SOS4 (pyridoxal kinase involved in salt stress) and CRK28 (cysteine-rich receptor-like kinase involved in calcium signaling), both significant in three studies.

AT4G17310 Splicing: This stress-responsive gene undergoes significant intron retention under multiple distinct extraterrestrial stress profiles, serving as a unified post-transcriptional marker of environmental stress in space biology.

03Machine Learning: Batch Effects & Generalization

UMAP and tree-based classification reveal that study-specific batch effects dominate biological signals.

Machine learning classification of the 199-sample × 9,875-gene Percent Spliced In (PSI) matrix achieves a pooled nested 5-fold CV AUC of 0.598 for predicting treatment (stress) versus control. However, 7-study Leave-One-Study-Out Cross-Validation (LOSOCV) performance drops to near-random (mean AUC ~0.41), showing poor cross-study generalization. Unsupervised UMAP embedding reveals that samples cluster almost perfectly by study identity (ARI = 0.995) rather than by treatment status (ARI = -0.002). This represents a critical methodological finding: batch effects overwhelmingly dominate the alternative splicing landscape in public space omics.

04Ontology & Functional Convergence

Circadian clock splicing is disrupted across all ISS flight studies, while substrates activate stressor-specific pathways.

Our multi-ontology enrichment analysis identified highly stressor-specific splicing adaptations:

  • Circadian Clock Disruption: Regulation of circadian rhythm was enriched in all four flight studies (OSD-314, OSD-120, OSD-37 via RVE1, and OSD-678). This reveals a post-transcriptional layer to the known circadian dysregulation observed in space.
  • Lunar Regolith (OSD-476): As the most splicing-responsive study (306 significant events), growth in lunar regolith induces extensive splicing alterations in photosynthesis light reactions, mitotic cell cycle, and telomeric regions.
  • Fractional Gravity (OSD-251): Splicing changes are heavily enriched for superoxide response (FDR=0.001, 488x fold enrichment), reflecting oxidative stress under partial gravity.
  • GCR Radiation (OSD-658): Enriches for phospholipid metabolism and stress-responsive translation pathways.
